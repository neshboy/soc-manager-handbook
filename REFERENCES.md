# References

Real, externally verified sources cited inline in the chapters (as `(Author/Organization, "Title," Publication/Standard, Year: URL)`), added during a scholarly-citation pass. Every source below was independently fetched and confirmed before citing — see `STYLE-GUIDE.md` §9 for how this book classifies figure/claim evidence (`OFFICIAL REFERENCE` = sourced from published external research, exactly what these citations back).

This list is additive: if a future revision adds another verified inline citation anywhere in the book, add it here once, in author-alphabetical order, rather than creating a second references file.

- Czeisler, Charles A.; Duffy, Jeanne F.; Shanahan, Theresa L.; Brown, Emery N.; et al. "Stability, Precision, and Near-24-Hour Period of the Human Circadian Pacemaker." *Science*, Vol. 284, No. 5423, 1999, pp. 2177–2181. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.284.5423.2177
  - Cited in: Part 17 §1.1, for the finding that the human circadian pacemaker's intrinsic period averages about 24.18 hours (not exactly 24), established via a forced-desynchrony protocol under controlled lighting.

- Czeisler, Charles A.; Moore-Ede, Martin C.; Coleman, Richard M. "Rotating Shift Work Schedules That Disrupt Sleep Are Improved by Applying Circadian Principles." *Science*, Vol. 217, No. 4558, 1982, pp. 460–463. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7089576
  - Cited in: Part 17 §1.3, for the field-study evidence behind forward (phase-delaying) vs. backward (phase-advancing) shift rotation, and the measured improvements in satisfaction, health, and productivity from redesigning a rotation around circadian principles.

- Federal Aviation Administration. "Basics of Aviation Fatigue." Advisory Circular 120-100, June 7, 2010. https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC%20120-100.pdf
  - Cited in: Part 17 §1.2, for the definition and clock-hour range of the "window of circadian low" (WOCL) used in aviation fatigue-risk-management practice.

- Sundaramurthy, Sathya Chandran; Bardas, Alexandru G.; Case, Jacob; Ou, Xinming; Wesch, Michael; McHugh, John; Rajagopalan, S. Raj. "A Human Capital Model for Mitigating Security Analyst Burnout." Proceedings of the Eleventh Symposium On Usable Privacy and Security (SOUPS 2015), USENIX, July 2015. https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2015/proceedings/presentation/sundaramurthy
  - Cited in: Part 17 "Why this part exists," for the field-study finding that SOC analyst burnout arises from the cyclic interaction of human, technical, and managerial factors, and that manager-controlled workflow/staffing/rotation changes — not individual coping alone — are what mitigate it.
